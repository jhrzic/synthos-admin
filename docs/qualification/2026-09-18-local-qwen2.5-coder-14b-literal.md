`cached_tokens`. The imported $0 record left the cached-input rate unstated (`null`). The spend guard refuses to guess
a rate it was not given, so it recorded those five actual costs as UNKNOWN instead of $0. No money was spent or could
have been: the route is FREE_LOCAL on loopback with no credential, every reservation was $0, and paid execution was
OFF throughout. But five of the six rows do **not** show a *calculated* $0.

**What else was wrong.** The executor's local-cost integrity check fell back to the $0 estimate when the actual cost
was unknown, so it passed those five rows. It should have failed them.

**Fixed after the run, in code (the ledger rows are history and were not rewritten):**
- The local-runtime importer now records `cachedInput: 0` explicitly.
- `approveLocalZeroPrice` refuses a record whose cached rate is not an explicit 0.
- The integrity check (`localZeroCostCheck`) requires a KNOWN actual cost of exactly $0 and never substitutes the
  estimate.
- The test double now reports cached tokens the way Ollama does.

**Consequence for this run.** The model's behaviour evidence is sound: six exact matches, each with its own decision,
Guardian verdict, ledger row, review and verified receipt. The cost evidence is incomplete for five rows. Whether to
